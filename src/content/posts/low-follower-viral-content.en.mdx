When a post from a big account goes viral, you can't tell how much of that performance came from the content itself and how much came from the account's existing audience. A creator with 500K followers posting a product review that gets 30K likes tells you very little — was it the topic, or was it the built-in traffic?

This creates a real problem: if you only study viral content from big accounts, you're learning the wrong lessons. You might copy a top creator's exact format, get 200 views on your new account, and conclude the topic doesn't work — when the real issue was that you don't have their follower base to begin with.

The algorithm doesn't discriminate against new accounts. Both Xiaohongshu (RedNote) and Douyin use a recommendation system that first exposes content to a small audience, then decides whether to push it further based on engagement rate. Follower count affects the initial exposure pool, but once content enters the recommendation loop, continued distribution depends entirely on how well it performs at each stage. A post from a small account with high likes means it beat the average engagement rate at every round — and the algorithm kept feeding it more traffic.

For new creators, the most useful reference material meets one condition: the account is roughly your size, but a single post far outperforms that account's average. That's evidence the topic itself is doing the heavy lifting, not the follower base.

## How to Find Viral Posts From Small Accounts in Bulk

You can do this manually, but it's painfully slow. You need both the like count of each post and the creator's follower count side by side to cross-filter. Platform search results don't show follower counts by default — you'd have to click into each profile one by one. A single page of search results could eat an hour.

A more efficient approach is to automate the search-and-filter workflow:

1. Search your niche keywords on Xiaohongshu or Douyin
2. Sort results by likes and scrape them in bulk
3. Enable the low-follower viral content filter during scraping, setting a follower cap (e.g., your own follower count as the upper limit)
4. Export and do a secondary filter by follower count to isolate low-follower, high-engagement samples

## What to Look for After Finding These Posts

Filtering is not the finish line — you need to extract patterns. Look at three things:

**What problem does the title address?** Line up the titles of these low-follower viral posts and you'll likely see them clustering around a few specific sub-topics. These are areas with strong demand but weak supply.

**What makes the content compelling?** Some posts win through raw authenticity (real mistakes, actual costs), others through information gaps (angles nobody else has covered). Understanding the source of their appeal tells you where to focus your own effort.

**What are people asking in the comments?** When you see repeated requests like "drop the link," "how did you do that," or "same situation here," it confirms the demand is real. You can also use [comment scraping](/xiaohongshu/comments) to export comments in bulk for analysis.

To turn this into a reviewable workflow, continue with [Xiaohongshu viral post analysis](/xiaohongshu/viral-content-analysis) or [Douyin viral video analysis](/douyin/viral-content-analysis). Each workflow connects low-follower discovery with a source-backed breakdown of the post, transcript, visuals, and public feedback.

## Which Viral Small-Account Posts Can't Be Replicated

Three types to exclude: posts that rode a breaking news cycle or platform meme — once the window closes, you can't recreate the conditions; posts that went viral because of the creator's personal story (a 25-year-old building a factory in Africa, a top-university dropout selling street food) — the hook is the person, not the topic; and posts from near-zero-follower accounts with thousands of likes but a dead comment section — likely anomalous data, discard them.
