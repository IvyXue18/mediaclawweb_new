Many content creators operate under a default assumption: what I think is important must be what my audience thinks is important too.

The books you've read, the skills you've built, the frameworks you've internalized — these shape what you think a post "should" cover. But users don't search based on your knowledge structure.

When someone opens the Xiaohongshu search bar, they're not thinking in frameworks. They're in a specific situation.

## A 5-Step Xiaohongshu Keyword Research Process

This can be broken down into a repeatable workflow.

### Step 1: Search Your Core Term and Read the Suggestions

Go to the Xiaohongshu search bar. Type in your niche's core keyword, but **don't press Enter** — just read the autocomplete suggestions that appear.

These suggestions aren't random. They reflect what real users search for most frequently. You'll almost certainly discover queries you never considered — real demand you didn't know existed.

### Step 2: A-Z Expansion — Uncover Every Long-Tail Keyword

The default suggestions only scratch the surface. After your core keyword, add a space, then type each letter from a to z (and digits 0–9), reading the suggestions each time.

<img
  src="https://obsidian-1316461920.cos.ap-chengdu.myqcloud.com/20260416124947862.png"
  alt="A-Z keyword expansion method on Xiaohongshu search bar"
/>

For example: "skincare routine a", "skincare routine b", and so on. Some suggestions will surprise you — queries you've never heard of, but the platform is telling you people are actively searching for them. A single seed keyword can expand into dozens or even hundreds of long-tail keywords.

### Step 3: Check the Top Results — Evaluate Content Quality

Pick a few interesting keywords, search for them, and review the top 10 posts. Look for these signals:

- Do the top-ranking posts actually answer the user's question?
- Are they well-crafted or low-effort? How's the engagement?
- What are users asking about in the comments? What are they complaining about?

Many people stop at finding keywords and start writing immediately. But the keyword is just an entry point — the quality of existing content determines whether there's an opportunity.

### Step 4: Classify by Competition — Find the Content Gaps

The keywords you find will generally fall into three categories:

| Category                       | What You'll See                                  | Strategy                                                                             |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| High demand, strong content    | Top posts are high-quality with good engagement  | Competitive — save for later                                                         |
| High demand, weak content      | Users are searching, but nobody's answering well | **Biggest opportunity — prioritize this**                                            |
| High demand, near-zero content | Very few search results                          | Proceed with caution — could be a blue ocean or a topic the platform doesn't promote |

The second category offers the best return on effort. Users are searching, nobody's writing well — you step in, and the search traffic is yours.

### Step 5: Focus on the User's Real Need, Not Keyword Stuffing

Once you've chosen a keyword, don't jump straight into outlining. First ask: **What problem is the person searching this keyword actually trying to solve?**

The search query is the entry point; the underlying need is the anchor for your content. Write around the need, not around cramming in keywords — that's the difference between content SEO and spam SEO.
