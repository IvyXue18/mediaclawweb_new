## 4 Types of Signals in the Comments Best Suited for Topic Mining

Not every comment is useful for generating topics—for instance, those generic "looks great" compliments. What you need are questions that appear repeatedly, point to specific needs, and relate directly to your business direction.

Here are the four categories you should pay the most attention to:

### High-Frequency Questions: Users Telling You What They Lack

For example, under a perfume review post, a comment with 141 likes asks, "Can you recommend a refreshing perfume for summer? Something that instantly cools you down." Another asks, "Any affordable perfumes around $15 for a woman in her 40s? I want to buy one for my mom." Someone else asks, "Any recommendations for perfumes with an aquatic, watery feel?" — When similar questions keep popping up, it means your existing content hasn't fully addressed these niche demands.

Every high-frequency question is a ready-made tutorial topic. The user has already articulated the need; if you write about it, you will hit the target precisely.

### Recurring Doubts: Your Content Opportunities

Under the same post, one person says, "This anti-aging serum is surprisingly good," while another comments, "It smells like an old lady." A third says, "I loved the top notes, but the base notes smell like ammonia." — The same product is being discussed repeatedly with polar opposite opinions. Another comment with 103 likes bluntly asks, "Is it possible that because you think it's expensive, you've convinced yourself it smells expensive?"

These aren't malicious comments; they represent a trust gap. There is only discussion when there are opposing views. This is the perfect opportunity to write myth-busting content, such as "A few pitfalls you might encounter with Product X," or an honest, balanced comparison highlighting its limitations. When done right, these types of posts often yield much higher conversion rates than purely promotional ones.

### Scenario-Based Queries: Your Content Granularity Isn't Fine Enough

"Is No. 3 suitable for guys?" "What's a good scent for someone with a sweet, cute style?" "Is there a perfume that smells like the baby oil or body wash we used as kids?" — These questions are telling you: your content's granularity is not fine enough. Users are projecting their own identities and scenarios onto the product, but your content hasn't covered them.

Every scenario-based query can be spun into a dedicated post targeting a specific audience or use case. "Niche perfumes guys can wear," "Affordable perfume gift guide for moms," "Refreshing summer scents" — the topics practically write themselves.

### Near-Decision Questions: High Commercial Value Topics

"Where did you buy this, sis?" "How do I buy Clinique? Through a proxy shopper?" "I can't find the Supreme Edition anywhere." "I'm sold on the first one, gonna buy a sample to try it out." "How much per bottle?" — These comments are both conversion signals and high-value commercial topics.

If many people are asking the exact same pre-purchase question, it means your current content is failing to assist them at the decision-making stage. Turning these questions into posts like "The most reliable places to buy these perfumes" or "How to buy starter samples without getting scammed" can directly shorten the user's decision path.

## The Complete Workflow: From Comments to Topic List in 4 Steps

### Step 1: Pick the Right Comment Samples

Not all comment sections are worth analyzing. You need to select posts that fit these criteria:

<img
  src="https://media.mediaclaw.app/blog-covers/23d0bcf3291bf2cf71a6e62e2123e6f0.png"
  alt="Filtering posts by comment count"
/>

- **Posts with high volume and genuine discussion.** A comment section with over 100 replies usually reveals clear patterns. If the section is just filled with "Great post" or "Saved," there's little analytical value.
- **Competitors' high-engagement content.** You don't only have to look at your own account. User questions in your competitors' comment sections are just as valuable for your own topic mining.
- **Content published within the last 3 months.** For older comment sections, users' focal points may have already shifted.

Selecting 3 to 5 posts is enough to get started. If you pick the wrong samples, the subsequent analysis will be a waste of time.

### Step 2: Batch Export Comments and Sync to Feishu (Lark)

Manually scrolling through the comment section has three major flaws: it's slow, you easily miss things, and it's impossible to review later. Copying and pasting 300 comments by hand takes at least half an hour, and by the end of it, you've forgotten what you saw at the beginning.

### Step 3: 3D AI Analysis for Automated Topic Discovery

This step delivers the biggest efficiency boost in the entire workflow. Once the comment data enters the Feishu Base, the built-in AI capabilities will automatically process it across three dimensions:

**Dimension 1: User Need Analysis**

The AI extracts genuine user needs from the comments. Each identified need includes:

- **Need Category** — Are they asking for a how-to method, expressing distrust, describing a specific scenario, or trying to make a purchase decision?
- **Specific Details** — What exactly is the user asking, caring about, struggling with, or joking about?
- **Frequency** — Is this need appearing at high frequency, medium frequency, or is it a rare but strong sentiment?

You no longer need to manually review hundreds of comments and categorize them by feel. The AI scans them once, and a full map of user needs is generated instantly.

**Dimension 2: Scalable Topic Discovery**

Based on the categorized needs, the AI automatically deduces 3 to 5 independent topic directions. Each entry includes:

- **Topic Title** — Can be used directly as your post's headline.
- **Content Angle** — Which perspective should this topic take?
- **Target Audience** — Whose exact problem is this topic solving?

You jump straight from "I have a bunch of comments and don't know what to write" to "These 5 topics can be scheduled for next week."

**Dimension 3: Topic Tracing**

For every deduced topic, the AI will tag which specific comment or cluster of comments inspired it, quoting key phrases from the original text.

This step solves the issue of "topic explainability"—you don't just know _what_ to write, but _why_ you are writing it. If someone in a meeting asks, "Where did this topic come from?", you can point directly to the user's exact words.

### Step 4: Prioritize and Schedule

Not every AI-generated topic needs to be written. The final prioritization is up to you. To judge whether a topic is worth prioritizing, check these three conditions:

**Highest Priority: High Frequency + Specific + Highly Relevant to Business.** For example, "How to batch export Xiaohongshu comments"—if you are building social media tools, this is high-frequency, specific, and directly related to your product. You must write it first.

**Secondary Priority: Clearly Matches a Real Use Case.** The user isn't asking abstract questions; they are describing their situation. "I run an education business, can I use this method for student enrollment?"—Content written for this type of question naturally possesses a strong sense of context, making it easy for readers to project themselves into it.

**Normal Priority: Valuable but Broad.** "Is there an easier way?" "Can you explain this in more detail?"—This feedback indicates that the granularity of your current explanations needs adjustment, but it doesn't necessarily warrant a standalone post. Use these to optimize existing content.

After filtering, slot the confirmed topics into your Feishu content calendar. AI solves the efficiency problem of moving "from comments to topics," but human judgment is still required for prioritization—don't let 10 great topic candidates sit in a spreadsheet and never get written.

## Keep the Flywheel Spinning, Don't Let It Gather Dust

A one-time analysis of 3 to 5 posts' comment sections can yield over a dozen candidate topics, which is enough to last two to three weeks. However, the value of the comments section is continuous—users' focal points shift, and new posts generate fresh discussions.

A sustainable minimum viable loop:

1. **Run the comment scraper once every week or two**, covering recently published high-engagement content (both yours and your competitors').
2. **Once new comments enter the Feishu Base, trigger the AI analysis** so that new needs and topics are automatically generated.
3. **Always pick from the topic pool before scheduling content**, instead of starting a new brainstorm. The topic pool is a consumable resource—cross off topics as you write them, and let new comments continuously replenish the pool.
