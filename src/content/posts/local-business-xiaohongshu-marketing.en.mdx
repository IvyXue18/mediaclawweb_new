## How "Local Posts" Actually Work on Xiaohongshu (RedNote)

Many people assume a post becomes "local" simply by adding a location tag. In reality, Xiaohongshu uses a two-layer system to determine local relevance.

**Layer 1: POI tagging.** When you add a specific location to your post — a landmark, a shopping district, a store address — the algorithm prioritizes showing it to nearby users. Choosing a specific venue or district rather than just a city name gives you denser local exposure.

**Layer 2: Location-related keywords and hashtags.** When your title, body text, or hashtags mention city names, district names, or local terms (e.g., "#HulunbuirGrassland" "#HailarFoodGuide"), the algorithm identifies the post's geographic relevance and decides whether to push it into the local feed.

You need both layers working together. A post with a POI tag but no location keywords in the text — or vice versa — only taps into half the available traffic.

For local businesses, the rule is simple: **always add a specific POI when publishing, and naturally weave city and district names into your copy.**

## The Tourism Exception: You Need Both Local and Nationwide Reach

Some local businesses — restaurants, nail salons, gyms — only need customers within a few kilometers. Studying local posts is enough. But tourism is different.

If you run tours in Hulunbuir, your customers are scattered across the entire country. Before they travel, they search for things like "Hulunbuir travel guide," "how much does a Hulunbuir trip cost," or "Hulunbuir family-friendly itinerary." These are nationwide search queries, not local ones. If you only focus on local posts, you'll only reach people who are already there — and miss the biggest pool of potential customers: **people who are still planning their trip.**

For tourism businesses on Xiaohongshu (RedNote), the strategy needs two tracks:

- **70% of effort on nationwide keyword content:** Cover search terms like "destination + guide / budget / duration / season" to capture users during the pre-trip decision stage
- **30% of effort studying local posts:** Reference how local creators write, how they tag POIs, and what on-the-ground perspectives resonate

The core principle: **write your content for a nationwide audience, but always publish with a specific POI.** This way, your posts get found through nationwide search traffic and shown to nearby users at the same time.
